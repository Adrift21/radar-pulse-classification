# Results Summary — 9 Experiments

Final test results, comparison figures, and interpretation for the 3 × 3 experiment matrix
(3 time–frequency representations × 3 architectures). This document is the direct source for the
manuscript's **Results** and **Discussion** sections.

- **Test set:** 6,000 samples (~750 per class), SNR-stratified, from the frozen split
  (`configs/splits.npz`). See [`dataset.md`](./dataset.md).
- **Raw data:** `experiments/results/<tf>_<arch>/test_metrics.json`
- **Figures and tables:** generated by the scripts in [`analysis/`](../analysis/)

## 3. Findings

### 3.1 Representation ranking: STFT > CWD ≫ WVD

STFT (~97.9%) and CWD (~97.4%) are close. The ~0.5-point gap between them is **small but
statistically real** at n = 6000 (STFT vs CWD at the Custom-CNN level: p = 0.013, §5) — a small
effect size, not noise. WVD trails at ~89%, and that ~9-point gap is overwhelmingly significant
(p < 1e-70). However, the overall average hides the real story: the gap comes almost entirely from
the low-SNR regime (§3.2).

### 3.2 WVD's low-SNR collapse (the paper's headline finding)

The gap between the STFT mean and the WVD mean changes sharply with SNR:

| SNR | STFT | WVD | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| −10 dB | 84.8 | 24.8 | **60.0** |
| −8 dB | 93.7 | 50.7 | 42.9 |
| −6 dB | 97.6 | 76.2 | 21.4 |
| −4 dB | 97.1 | 87.9 | 9.3 |
| −2 dB | 98.5 | 95.3 | 3.2 |
| 0 dB | 98.1 | 96.2 | 1.9 |
| +2 dB | 99.2 | 98.6 | 0.6 |
| ≥ +4 dB | ~99.6 | ~99.3 | < 1 |

At −10 dB, WVD reaches only **24.8%** — barely above the 12.5% chance level for 8 classes, i.e.
close to random. From +2 dB upward the three representations are practically indistinguishable
(~99%). The convergence point is ≈ 0 to +2 dB.

### 3.3 Interpretation — cross-term interference (Discussion draft)

The WVD is a quadratic time–frequency distribution and therefore generates **cross-terms** by
construction. Even though our signals are mono-component (one radar class per sample), the added
AWGN creates noise×signal and noise×noise cross-terms. Because these terms scale with signal
energy, they spread across the entire time–frequency plane and drown the authentic signature at
low SNR. The STFT (linear, windowed, produces no cross-terms) and the CWD (whose Choi–Williams
kernel suppresses cross-terms at σ = 1.0) are far more resilient to this degradation. At high SNR
(≥ +2 dB) the noise-induced cross-terms become negligible and the WVD's superior time–frequency
resolution reasserts itself — which is why all three representations converge there.

**Visual evidence (`qualitative_tf_{lfm,costas}.png`).** Comparing the same signal across
representations as SNR drops makes the mechanism visible directly. At +20 dB the WVD signature
(the LFM diagonal, or the Costas frequency-hop staircase) is the sharpest of the three; by −6 dB it
is fading; and at −10 dB it is buried in a bright cross-term/noise speckle that fills the whole
time–frequency plane. The STFT's diagonal/staircase and the CWD's (kernel-suppressed) signature
remain readable even at −10 dB. This is the direct visual counterpart of the −10 dB accuracy gap in
§3.2 (WVD ~25% vs STFT/CWD ~85%).

**Concluding statement.** In low-SNR environments such as electronic warfare, the raw WVD's
resolution advantage is more than offset by noise-driven cross-term contamination; the STFT and the
CWD are markedly more robust across the operationally meaningful SNR range.

### 3.4 Architecture is secondary — resource efficiency

Within each representation family the spread across architectures is ≤ 1 point:
Custom-CNN ≈ ResNet-50 ≳ ViT-Small. The lightweight custom CNN matches the far heavier ResNet-50
and ViT-Small — and in fact holds the single highest score (98.22% on STFT). Model complexity
quantifies this:

| Model | Params (M) | vs Custom | GFLOPs @224² | GMACs | Best Test Acc (STFT) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Custom-CNN** | **1.77** | **1.0×** | **5.35** | **2.67** | **98.22** |
| ResNet-50 | 23.52 | 13.3× | 8.02 | 4.01 | 98.00 |
| ViT-Small | 21.47 | 12.1× | 8.40 | 4.20 | 97.45 |

> Source: `analysis/model_complexity.py` (built through the project's model registry, 1×224×224
> input, `pretrained=False` — the counts are weight-independent). FLOPs measured with torch's
> `FlopCounterMode` (conv + linear + attention matmuls). GMACs = GFLOPs / 2.

The Custom-CNN delivers the highest accuracy with ~13× fewer parameters and ~1.5× fewer FLOPs. An
ImageNet-scale backbone (ResNet-50 / ViT-Small) is therefore **unnecessary** for this 8-class
problem: a small, task-specific CNN is both more accurate and far cheaper. That matters
operationally on constrained hardware and for any edge deployment.

**Note on significance.** "Architecture is secondary" is true of the *effect size* (≤ ~1 point) but
not of statistical significance: a paired McNemar test on WVD shows the Custom-CNN is
**significantly better** than both ResNet-50 (p < 0.001) and ViT-Small (p = 0.029) (§5). The
lightweight model is not merely competitive — where we can test it pairwise, it is significantly
the best.

### 3.5 Per-class observations

The hardest classes are generally LFM (lowest precision, ~0.93–0.96; confused with the
NLFM/discretised-chirp families) and SteppedFH (notably low recall under WVD). Frank, Polyphase, and
CW are the easiest across all conditions (F1 ≈ 0.99). Details are in each experiment's
`confusion_matrix.png` and `class_snr_accuracy.png`.

## 4. WVD Low-SNR Confusion Analysis

> Source: `analysis/wvd_lowsnr_confusion.py`, using the per-sample `labels`/`preds`/`snr` arrays in
> `eval_arrays.npz`. Figure: `analysis/wvd_lowsnr_confusion_custom_cnn.png`.
>
> **Important limitation:** this analysis was only possible for WVD. The STFT/CWD checkpoints
> (`.pth`, large and gitignored) were pruned from disk, so their per-sample predictions cannot be
> recovered without retraining (see §6). WVD is the representation that collapses, so it is the
> case of interest anyway.

WVD's errors **change in character with SNR** — there is no single fixed confusion pair.

**(a) −10 dB — diffuse, noise/cross-term-driven confusion.** Accuracy ~25%. No pair dominates: even
the strongest are only ~22–34%, and predictions smear broadly toward the **frequency-agile "sink"
classes** (the Costas, SteppedFH, and NLFM columns). This is the direct signature of noise-induced
cross-terms filling the WVD plane with broadband patterns, which the model reads as
frequency-hopping classes. Per-class recall (pooled over the 3 architectures):

| Class | Recall @ −10 dB | Class | Recall @ −10 dB |
|---|---|---|---|
| LFM | **9.6%** (worst) | Costas | 28.7% |
| Barker | 19.4% | SteppedFH | 30.2% |
| NLFM | 21.1% | Polyphase | 30.6% |
| CW | 24.4% | Frank | **34.0%** (best) |

**(b) −8 → −6 dB — transition to structural confusion.** As the noise recedes, errors concentrate on
the **genuinely similar** pairs: LFM ↔ NLFM (the chirp family; 27% at −8 dB, 15%/13% at −6 dB) and
Frank ↔ Polyphase (the matrix-phase family; ~11–24%). By −6 dB the matrix is nearly diagonal
(accuracy ~76%).

**Hypothesised pairs, confirmed — but in different regimes.** Both anticipated confusion pairs do
appear, in **different SNR regimes**: Costas ↔ SteppedFH is a **−10 dB (noise) phenomenon**
(SteppedFH→Costas 24%, Costas→SteppedFH 23%), whereas LFM ↔ NLFM is a **structural (−8/−6 dB)
phenomenon**. The answer to "what does the model confuse at low SNR?" is therefore SNR-dependent:
first noise-driven attraction, then intra-family similarity.

## 5. Statistical Significance

> Source: `analysis/statistical_significance.py`. Figure: `analysis/overall_accuracy_ci.png`.
>
> **How the data constraint determines the method.** Per-sample predictions exist only for WVD
> (the STFT/CWD checkpoints were pruned, §4). Therefore: (i) overall-accuracy CIs for all nine
> models use the **analytic Wilson interval** (which needs only n and accuracy); (ii) comparisons
> *between representations* use an **unpaired two-proportion z-test** — a paired test would be more
> powerful, so the reported p-values are **conservative**; (iii) the *architecture* comparison uses a
> **paired McNemar test** within WVD (identical test-sample ordering was verified).
>
> **Note on what these intervals do and do not cover.** They quantify **test-set sampling error
> only**. They do **not** capture training-time randomness (weight init, batch order, dropout,
> augmentation RNG). Small differences — notably STFT vs CWD, and the best architecture — would need
> a multi-seed study to be fully defensible; see §6.

**Bootstrap validation.** On WVD (where per-sample data exists), a 5,000-resample percentile
bootstrap CI matches the analytic Wilson CI **to two decimal places** (e.g. WVD/Custom-CNN:
bootstrap [88.78, 90.33] vs Wilson [88.77, 90.32]). This justifies using Wilson for STFT/CWD.

**Overall accuracy, 95% Wilson CI (n = 6000):**

| TF | Custom-CNN | ResNet-50 | ViT-Small |
|---|---|---|---|
| STFT | 98.22 [97.85, 98.52] | 98.00 [97.61, 98.32] | 97.45 [97.02, 97.82] |
| CWD | 97.57 [97.15, 97.93] | 97.55 [97.13, 97.91] | 97.15 [96.70, 97.54] |
| WVD | 89.57 [88.77, 90.32] | 88.43 [87.60, 89.22] | 88.85 [88.03, 89.62] |

**(a) Representation effect (architecture fixed = Custom-CNN), unpaired two-proportion z-test:**

| Comparison | Δ (points) | p | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| STFT vs CWD | +0.65 | 0.013 | significant (small effect) |
| STFT vs WVD | +8.65 | 4e-87 | overwhelmingly significant |
| CWD vs WVD | +8.00 | 2e-71 | overwhelmingly significant |

**(b) Architecture effect (representation fixed = WVD), paired McNemar:**

| Comparison | Discordant (a/b) | p | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Custom-CNN vs ResNet-50 | 204 / 136 | < 0.001 | significant — Custom-CNN better |
| Custom-CNN vs ViT-Small | 206 / 163 | 0.029 | significant — Custom-CNN better |
| ResNet-50 vs ViT-Small | 161 / 186 | 0.198 | not significant |

**Interpretation.** With n = 6000, even small differences become statistically detectable, so the
distinction between "is it significant?" and "does it matter (effect size)?" is essential. The
findings: STFT is significantly the best representation (by ~0.5 points over CWD, ~9 points over
WVD), and the Custom-CNN is significantly the best architecture where it can be tested pairwise. The
STFT/CWD confidence intervals are entirely disjoint from WVD's as a cluster, i.e. **the choice of
representation matters far more than the choice of architecture.**

### 5.1 Multi-seed results (training-variance)

> Source: `experiments/run_multiseed.py` (3 seeds: 42, 43, 44) + `experiments/aggregate_seeds.py`.
> Figure: `analysis/multiseed_accuracy.png`; table: `analysis/seed_summary.csv`. Protocol: the
> seed varies weight init + data ordering; the frozen split and val/test noise are held fixed.

Overall test accuracy, **mean ± std over 3 seeds**:

| TF | Custom-CNN | ResNet-50 | ViT-Small |
|---|---|---|---|
| STFT | **98.16 ± 0.07** | 98.10 ± 0.11 | 97.95 ± 0.43 |
| CWD | 97.42 ± 0.13 | 97.53 ± 0.03 | 96.84 ± 0.27 |
| WVD | 89.79 ± 0.20 | 88.81 ± 0.35 | 88.17 ± 1.28 |

Representation means (pooled over arch × seed): STFT **98.07 ± 0.24**, CWD **97.26 ± 0.36**,
WVD **88.92 ± 0.97**.

**Key outcomes — the fine-grained claims survive the multi-seed test:**
1. **STFT > CWD is real, not seed noise.** Δmean = +0.81 pt > combined std 0.60 pt → the two
   representation clusters are separable across seeds. (Single-seed §5(a) had said +0.65 pt; the
   3-seed result confirms it.)
2. **Representation clusters stay disjoint** (STFT/CWD ≫ WVD), as expected — that gap is huge.
3. **ViT-Small is the least stable architecture** (std up to 1.28 on WVD vs ≤0.20 for Custom-CNN),
   consistent with transformers' sensitivity to optimisation on limited data. Custom-CNN and
   ResNet-50 are very stable (std ≤ 0.13 on STFT/CWD).
4. **Custom-CNN is best or statistically tied at every representation** and the most seed-stable —
   nuancing the earlier "significantly best" (seed-42 McNemar) into "best/tied, and most stable",
   while the ~13× parameter advantage is unchanged.
